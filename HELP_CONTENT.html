Introduction

Welcome to Financial Projector! This comprehensive financial modeling tool helps you plan, project, and analyze your financial future. With powerful features for tracking assets, liabilities, income, and expenses, you can create detailed financial projections and visualize your wealth over time.

This help guide will walk you through all the features and functionality available in Financial Projector. Use the table of contents above to navigate to specific topics, or browse through each section to get a complete understanding of the application.

Getting Started

To get the most out of Financial Projector, we recommend:

  1. Setting up your profile information in Settings > Profile
  2. Configuring your categories in Settings > Categories
  3. Adding your accounts in Settings > Accounts
  4. Entering your assets, liabilities, income, and expenses in the My Data section
  5. Exploring the Dashboard to view your financial projections
Note: The more accurate data you enter, the more reliable your financial projections will be. Take time to enter all relevant financial information for the best results.

Authorized Users - Sharing Your Financial Data

You can grant other users access to view or edit your financial data. This is useful for:

  • Family members: Your spouse can view your financial projections
  • Advisors: Your financial advisor can view your assets and liabilities
  • Trusted individuals: Children or others you want to share specific data with

How to Authorize a User

Go to Settings > Authorized Users in the sidebar and click "+ Add Authorized User".

  1. Enter the user's email address
  2. (Optional) If the user is not yet registered, you can create their account by providing a temporary password. This is especially useful when you want someone to only view your data (see "View-Only Users" below).
  3. Set permissions for each area:
    • Accounts: Access to account settings
    • Items: Assets, liabilities, income, and expenses
    • Projections: Balance sheet and cash flow projections
    • Charts: Custom charts and visualizations
    • Documents: Document vault access
    Each permission can be set to "View" (read-only), "Edit" (can make changes), or "None" (no access).
  4. Click "Add User"

Two Types of Authorized Access

Situation 1: Users with Their Own Data

Users who have their own financial data can view your data AND their own data. They can switch between accounts using the account switcher in Settings. This is ideal for:

  • Financial advisors who manage multiple clients
  • Couples who each maintain their own financial data but want to view each other's
  • Family members who want to share data while maintaining their own accounts

Situation 2: View-Only Users

Users who don't have their own financial data will automatically see your data when they log in. This is ideal for:

  • Spouses who want to view but not manage the primary account holder's data
  • Family members who only need to view data, not create their own
  • Anyone you want to grant access to without them creating their own account

Creating View-Only Users: When you authorize a user who doesn't exist yet, you can provide a temporary password. The system will:

  • Create their account automatically
  • Send them an email with login credentials
  • Require them to change their password on first login
  • Automatically show them your data when they log in

Managing Authorized Users

  • View Authorized Users: See who you've granted access to in the "Users I've Authorized" tab
  • View Your Access: See who has granted you access in the "Access I've Received" tab
  • Edit Permissions: Click the edit icon next to any authorized user to change their permissions
  • Remove Access: Click the delete icon to revoke access

Switching Between Accounts

If you have access to multiple accounts (your own and authorized accounts), you can switch between them:

  1. Go to Settings > Switch Account
  2. Select the account you want to view
  3. All data (assets, liabilities, income, expenses, projections, charts) will update to show that account's data

Security Notes

  • Temporary passwords are sent via email - ensure you share them securely
  • Users must change their temporary password on first login
  • You can revoke access at any time
  • Permission changes take effect immediately
  • View-Only users cannot create their own data unless they switch to their own account (if they have one)

Document Vault

The Document Vault is a secure storage area for your important financial documents. Organize documents by creating folders and upload files for easy access.

Uploading Documents

  1. Go to My Data > Document Vault
  2. Navigate to the folder where you want to upload the document (or stay in the root folder)
  3. Click "Upload Document" or "+ Upload"
  4. Select the file from your computer
  5. The document will be uploaded and appear in the current folder

Organizing with Folders

Create folders to organize your documents:

  1. Click "New Folder" or "+ Folder"
  2. Enter a folder name
  3. Click "Create"
  4. Double-click a folder to enter it and view its contents

Managing Documents

  • View: Click on a document to view or download it
  • Rename: Click the rename icon to change a document or folder name
  • Delete: Click the delete icon to remove a document or folder (this action cannot be undone)
  • Move: Use the move feature to organize documents into different folders

Document Security

  • All documents are stored securely in the cloud
  • Only you and users you've authorized (with document permissions) can access your documents
  • Documents are encrypted in transit and at rest
  • Be mindful of file sizes - very large files may take longer to upload
Tip: Create folders for different categories like "Tax Documents", "Insurance Policies", "Investment Statements", etc., to keep your documents organized.

Categories

Categories help you organize and group your financial items. Use categories to track different types of assets, liabilities, income, and expenses.

Managing Categories

Go to Settings > Categories to manage your categories for:

  • Assets: Types of assets (e.g., "Retirement Accounts", "Real Estate", "Stocks")
  • Liabilities: Types of debts (e.g., "Mortgage", "Credit Cards", "Auto Loans")
  • Income: Sources of income (e.g., "Salary", "Investment Income", "Rental Income")
  • Expenses: Types of expenses (e.g., "Housing", "Food", "Transportation", "Taxes")

Adding Categories

  1. Select the category type (Assets, Liabilities, Income, or Expenses)
  2. Click "Add Category" or "+ New Category"
  3. Enter the category name
  4. Click "Save"

Editing and Deleting Categories

  • Edit: Click the edit icon next to a category to change its name
  • Delete: Click the delete icon to remove a category
Warning: Deleting a category that is in use by existing items may affect how those items are displayed or grouped. Consider renaming the category instead if items are using it.

Using Categories

When you add or edit an asset, liability, income, or expense item, you can assign it to a category. This helps with:

  • Organizing and filtering items in lists and charts
  • Grouping related items together
  • Creating category-based reports and projections
  • Understanding your financial structure at a glance

Default Categories

The system comes with default categories to get you started. You can modify, add to, or remove these as needed. Default categories are designed to cover common financial items, but feel free to customize them to match your specific needs.

Assets

Assets represent things you own that have value. Track your assets to understand your net worth and plan for the future.

Adding an Asset

  1. Go to My Data > Assets
  2. Click "Add Asset" or "+ New Asset"
  3. Fill in the asset details:
    • Name: A descriptive name for the asset
    • Category: Select from your asset categories
    • Account: Select which account this asset belongs to
    • Beginning Balance: The current value of the asset
    • Growth Rate: Expected annual growth percentage
    • Start Date: When the asset begins contributing to projections
    • End Date: (Optional) When the asset stops contributing
  4. For investment assets, you can also configure:
    • Dividend Rate: Annual dividend percentage
    • Reinvest Dividends: Whether dividends are reinvested or treated as income
    • Interest Rate: For fixed-income investments
  5. For retirement accounts, you can specify:
    • Internal Growth Rate: Base growth rate
    • Interest Rate: Interest component
    • Dividend Rate: Dividend component
    • The system will combine these into a total growth rate
  6. Click "Save" or "Create Asset"

Types of Assets

  • Cash Accounts: Checking, savings, money market accounts
  • Investment Accounts: Stocks, bonds, mutual funds, ETFs
  • Retirement Accounts: 401(k), IRA, pension accounts
  • Real Estate: Primary residence, rental properties, land
  • Other Assets: Vehicles, collectibles, business interests

Growth and Projections

Assets grow over time based on their growth rate. The system projects:

  • How asset values change over time
  • Growth based on compound interest/growth rates
  • How dividend and interest income affects asset values
  • Whether dividends are reinvested or flow to income

Managing Assets

  • Edit: Click the edit icon to modify asset details
  • Delete: Click the delete icon to remove an asset
  • View in Charts: Assets appear in your Net Worth and Cash Flow projections
Tip: For more accurate projections, use realistic growth rates. Historical averages: stocks ~7-10% annually, bonds ~3-5%, real estate varies by location.

Liabilities / Debts

Liabilities represent money you owe. Track your debts to understand your financial obligations and plan for repayment.

Adding a Liability

  1. Go to My Data > Liabilities / Debts
  2. Click "Add Liability" or "+ New Liability"
  3. Fill in the liability details:
    • Name: A descriptive name (e.g., "Mortgage", "Credit Card", "Car Loan")
    • Category: Select from your liability categories
    • Account: Select which account this liability belongs to
    • Beginning Balance: The current amount owed
    • Interest Rate: Annual interest rate on the debt
    • Minimum Payment: Required monthly payment
    • Payment Frequency: How often payments are made (monthly, annually, etc.)
    • Start Date: When the liability begins in projections
    • End Date: (Optional) When the liability is paid off or ends
  4. Click "Save" or "Create Liability"

Types of Liabilities

  • Mortgages: Home loans (may be auto-generated if connected to an account - see Auto-Generation)
  • Credit Cards: Revolving credit debt
  • Auto Loans: Vehicle financing
  • Student Loans: Education debt
  • Personal Loans: Other installment loans
  • Other Debts: Medical bills, tax debts, etc.

Mortgage Auto-Generation

If you have a mortgage connected through Plaid, the system can automatically generate mortgage payment expenses. See the Auto-Generation section for details.

Liability Projections

The system projects:

  • How your debt balances change over time
  • Interest costs based on your interest rates
  • Principal reduction based on payments
  • When debts will be paid off (if applicable)

Managing Liabilities

  • Edit: Click the edit icon to modify liability details
  • Delete: Click the delete icon to remove a liability
  • View Impact: Liabilities appear in Net Worth calculations and affect your overall financial position
Tip: Keep interest rates and payment amounts up to date for accurate projections. The system uses these to calculate interest expenses and principal reduction over time.

Income (Cash In)

Track all sources of income to understand your cash flow and plan for future income changes.

Adding Income

  1. Go to My Data > Income (Cash In)
  2. Click "Add Income" or "+ New Income"
  3. Fill in the income details:
    • Description: Source of income (e.g., "Salary", "Rental Income", "Dividend Income")
    • Category: Select from your income categories
    • Account: Select which account receives this income
    • Amount: Income amount per period
    • Frequency: How often you receive this income (monthly, annually, etc.)
    • Growth Rate: Expected annual increase (e.g., 3% for inflation-adjusted raises)
    • Start Date: When this income begins
    • End Date: (Optional) When this income stops (e.g., retirement date)
  4. For investment income, you can also set:
    • Reinvest Dividends: Whether dividends are reinvested in the asset or treated as cash income
  5. Click "Save" or "Create Income"

Types of Income

  • Salary/Wages: Regular employment income
  • Investment Income: Dividends, interest (may be auto-generated from assets)
  • Rental Income: Property rental payments
  • Business Income: Self-employment or business revenue
  • Retirement Income: Pension, Social Security, retirement withdrawals
  • Other Income: Any other cash inflow

Auto-Generated Income

Some income sources can be automatically generated:

  • Dividend Income: Generated from investment assets with dividend rates
  • Interest Income: Generated from interest-bearing assets

These auto-generated items appear in your income list with a blue highlight. See the Auto-Generation section for more details.

Investment Income and Reinvestment

For investment assets, you can choose whether dividends are:

  • Reinvested: Dividends are added back to the asset value (compound growth)
  • Paid Out: Dividends flow as income to your cash accounts

This choice affects both your asset growth projections and your cash flow projections.

Managing Income

  • Edit: Click the edit icon to modify income details
  • Delete: Click the delete icon to remove an income source
  • View in Cash Flow: All income appears in Cash Flow projections
Tip: Consider including future income changes like promotions, raises, or expected retirement date changes for more accurate long-term projections.

Net Worth

The Net Worth projection shows how your total wealth changes over time. It's calculated as Assets minus Liabilities.

Viewing Net Worth

Go to Dashboard > Net Worth to see:

  • A chart showing your net worth over the projection period
  • Individual asset values over time
  • Individual liability balances over time
  • A table with year-by-year details

Understanding the Chart

  • Net Worth Line: Shows your total net worth (all assets minus all liabilities)
  • Asset Lines: Individual asset values over time
  • Liability Lines: Individual debt balances over time

Reading the Table

The table shows year-by-year values for:

  • Beginning balance for each asset and liability
  • Ending balance for each asset and liability
  • Total assets and total liabilities
  • Net worth

Customizing the View

  • Projection Years: Set in Application Settings to control how many years forward to project
  • Export: Export the chart or table as PDF or CSV
  • Zoom: Use chart controls to zoom in on specific time periods

What Affects Net Worth

Your net worth projection is influenced by:

  • Asset growth rates
  • Liability interest rates and payments
  • New assets or liabilities added over time
  • Assets or liabilities that end (based on end dates)
  • Changes in asset values (growth)
  • Principal reduction on debts
Tip: A positive and growing net worth indicates improving financial health. Use this projection to set financial goals and track progress toward them.

Cash Flow

The Cash Flow projection shows how money flows into and out of your accounts over time. This is essential for understanding your liquidity and planning for future cash needs.

Viewing Cash Flow

Go to Dashboard > Cash Flow to see:

  • A BASE model chart showing Cash In, Cash Out, and Available Cash
  • A Sankey diagram visualizing cash flow between accounts and categories
  • Detailed tables with year-by-year cash flow breakdowns

BASE Model

The BASE model shows:

  • Cash In (Additions): All income sources flowing into your accounts
  • Cash Out (Subtractions): All expenses flowing out of your accounts
  • Available Cash: Beginning balance + Cash In - Cash Out = Ending balance
  • Total Assets: (Optional) Your total asset value over time

Y-Axis Options

For the BASE model chart, you can choose:

  • Separate Y-Axis: Available Cash uses a different scale (useful when cash amounts are much larger than income/expenses)
  • Single Y-Axis: All data series use the same scale (easier to compare relative sizes)

Sankey Diagram

The Sankey diagram provides a visual representation of:

  • How money flows from income sources to accounts
  • How money flows from accounts to expense categories
  • The relative size of different income and expense streams

Understanding Cash Flow

Positive cash flow means you're receiving more than you're spending. Negative cash flow means you're spending more than you're receiving. Monitor this to:

  • Ensure you have enough cash to cover expenses
  • Plan for major expenses or investments
  • Identify opportunities to save or invest excess cash
  • Understand when you might need to draw from savings or borrow

What Affects Cash Flow

Your cash flow is influenced by:

  • All income sources
  • All expense items
  • Automatic transfers between accounts
  • Surplus asset transfers
  • Tax calculations (auto-generated)
  • Automatic disbursements

Export Options

  • Export charts as PDF or images
  • Export tables as CSV for further analysis in spreadsheet software
Tip: Regularly review your cash flow projections to identify potential cash shortfalls early and plan accordingly. A good cash flow projection can help you avoid liquidity problems.

Monte Carlo

The Monte Carlo simulation runs thousands of projections with random variations to show the range of possible financial outcomes. This helps you understand uncertainty and plan for different scenarios.

Understanding Monte Carlo

Traditional projections use fixed growth rates and assumptions. Monte Carlo simulations:

  • Vary growth rates randomly within specified ranges
  • Run thousands of simulations
  • Show the probability of different outcomes
  • Help you understand worst-case, best-case, and most-likely scenarios

How to Run a Monte Carlo Simulation

  1. Go to Dashboard > Monte Carlo
  2. Configure simulation parameters:
    • Number of Simulations: How many random projections to run (more = more accurate but slower)
    • Confidence Intervals: Select which probability ranges to display (e.g., 90%, 75%, 50%)
    • Variation Ranges: Set how much asset growth rates can vary
  3. Click "Run Simulation"
  4. Review the results showing different outcome probabilities

Reading Monte Carlo Results

Results typically show:

  • Probability Bands: Shaded areas showing ranges of likely outcomes
  • Median (50th percentile): The most likely outcome
  • Best Case (90th percentile): Better outcomes (top 10%)
  • Worst Case (10th percentile): Worse outcomes (bottom 10%)

Using Monte Carlo Results

Use Monte Carlo simulations to:

  • Understand the range of possible financial outcomes
  • Plan for uncertainty and market volatility
  • Assess the risk of running out of money in retirement
  • Make more informed financial decisions
  • Stress-test your financial plan

Interpreting Results

  • If most simulations show positive outcomes, you're in good shape
  • If many simulations show negative outcomes, consider adjusting your plan
  • Wider probability bands indicate more uncertainty
  • Narrower bands indicate more predictable outcomes
Tip: Run Monte Carlo simulations periodically, especially when market conditions change or when you're making major financial decisions. This helps ensure your plan remains viable under different scenarios.

Custom Charts and Tables

Create custom charts and tables to visualize specific aspects of your financial data. Custom charts let you focus on particular assets, liabilities, income sources, or expenses.

Creating a Custom Chart

  1. Go to Dashboard > Custom
  2. Click "Create New Chart" or "+ New Chart"
  3. Enter a chart name
  4. Add data series:
    • Select a Data Type: Assets, Liabilities, Income, or Expenses
    • Choose whether to Itemize: Show individual items or aggregate totals
    • If itemized, select specific items or categories to include
    • Set a Label for the series (how it appears in the chart)
    • Add multiple series to compare different data points
  5. Click "Save" to create the chart

Chart Types

  • Line Charts: Show trends over time
  • Bar Charts: Compare values across categories or years
  • Combination Charts: Mix line and bar series in one chart

Customizing Charts

  • Add Series: Click "Add Series" to add more data to the chart
  • Edit Series: Click the edit icon to modify a series
  • Remove Series: Click the remove icon to delete a series
  • Change Itemization: Switch between showing individual items or aggregated totals
  • Refresh Itemization: Update item labels if underlying data has changed

Itemization

When you itemize a series, you can:

  • Show All Items: Display every item in a category
  • Show Specific Items: Select only certain items to display
  • Filter by Category: Show all items in a specific category

Use "Refresh Itemization" if item names or IDs have changed to update labels without recalculating the chart.

Viewing Charts

Charts show:

  • A visual chart/graph
  • A detailed table with year-by-year values
  • Options to export as PDF or CSV
  • Controls to zoom and navigate the chart

Recalculating Charts

  • Recalculate Individual Chart: Click "Recalculate" on a specific chart to update it with latest data
  • Recalculate All Charts: Use the "Recalculate All Charts" button to update every chart at once
  • Recalculation is needed when underlying data (assets, liabilities, income, expenses) has changed

Managing Charts

  • Edit: Click the edit icon to modify chart configuration
  • Delete: Click the delete icon to remove a chart
  • Duplicate: Create a copy of an existing chart as a starting point

Duplicate Chart Names

The system prevents creating charts with duplicate names. If you want to create a similar chart, use a unique name or edit an existing chart instead.

Tip: Create multiple custom charts for different purposes: one showing all retirement accounts, another showing all housing expenses, etc. This lets you drill down into specific aspects of your finances.

Auto-Generation

Financial Projector can automatically generate certain income and expense items based on your data and settings. This saves time and ensures calculations are based on your actual financial information.

Auto-Generated Items

The following items can be automatically generated:

Federal Income Tax

  • What it is: Calculated federal income tax based on your total income
  • How it works: Uses current federal tax brackets and your filing status (from Profile Settings)
  • Where it appears: In your Expenses list with a blue highlight
  • How to enable: Enabled by default in Application Settings
  • How to disable: Go to Settings > Application Settings and uncheck "Calculate Federal Income Tax"

State Income Tax

  • What it is: Calculated state income tax based on your income and state
  • How it works: Uses your state's tax brackets (from Profile Settings) and your filing status
  • Where it appears: In your Expenses list with a blue highlight, separate from federal tax
  • How to enable: Go to Settings > Application Settings and check "Calculate State Income Tax"
  • Requirements: You must set your state in Profile Settings for this to work
Important Tax Notes:
  • Tax calculations are estimates only and do not address the many variables that can affect taxes (deductions, credits, exemptions, etc.)
  • These calculations are for planning purposes only
  • Please consult a tax professional for detailed tax advice
  • This application does not address multi-state or partial residency - it assumes you live in your state the entire year
  • You can set the tax year in Application Settings to use the appropriate tax brackets for that year

Mortgage Payments

  • What it is: Principal and interest payments for mortgages connected via Plaid
  • How it works: Extracts payment information from connected mortgage accounts
  • Where it appears: In your Expenses list
  • Requirements: Must have a mortgage account connected through Plaid (see Connected Accounts)

Principal Reduction

  • What it is: The portion of mortgage payments that reduces the loan principal
  • How it works: Calculated from mortgage payment schedules
  • Where it appears: Affects liability balances in projections
  • Requirements: Requires connected mortgage account via Plaid

Interest and Dividend Income

  • What it is: Income generated from investment assets
  • How it works: Calculated from asset interest rates and dividend rates
  • Where it appears: In your Income list with a blue highlight
  • Requirements: Assets must have interest or dividend rates configured
  • Reinvestment Option: You can choose to reinvest dividends in the asset or receive them as cash income

Identifying Auto-Generated Items

Auto-generated items are highlighted in blue in your Income and Expenses lists. When you hover over them, you'll see a tooltip indicating they are auto-generated.

Managing Auto-Generated Items

  • You cannot directly edit auto-generated items
  • To change them, modify the underlying data (tax settings, asset rates, etc.)
  • Auto-generated items are recalculated when you run projections or recalculate charts
  • You can enable or disable tax calculations in Application Settings

How Auto-Generation Works

Auto-generated items are calculated during projection runs based on:

  • Your current settings (tax year, state, filing status)
  • Your income and expense data
  • Asset and liability configurations
  • Connected account information (for mortgage data)
Tip: Keep your profile settings (state, filing status) and asset configurations up to date to ensure accurate auto-generated calculations. Review auto-generated items periodically to ensure they match your expectations.

Surplus Asset Transfer

When you have excess cash in an account, you can automatically transfer it to another asset (like an investment account). This helps model automatic investment strategies.

Setting Up Surplus Asset Transfer

  1. Go to Settings > Automatic Transfers
  2. Navigate to the "Surplus Asset" tab
  3. Select the Source Account: The cash account where surplus accumulates
  4. Select the Target Asset: The asset to receive the surplus transfers
  5. Set transfer frequency and timing options
  6. Click "Save"

How It Works

The system monitors your source account for surplus cash (positive balance beyond a threshold). When surplus is detected, it automatically transfers to your target asset according to your settings.

Transfer Options

  • Transfer Frequency: How often transfers occur (monthly, quarterly, annually, etc.)
  • Transfer Amount: Fixed amount or all surplus above a threshold
  • Transfer Timing: When during the period the transfer occurs

Impact on Projections

Surplus transfers affect:

  • Cash Flow: Money flows from source account to target asset
  • Asset Growth: Target asset grows as it receives transfers
  • Liquidity: Source account balance is reduced, affecting available cash

Use Cases

  • Automatically invest excess cash in a brokerage account
  • Move surplus savings to a higher-yield investment
  • Model dollar-cost averaging strategies
  • Simulate automatic savings plans
Tip: Surplus asset transfers help ensure your cash doesn't sit idle. Use this feature to model automatic investment strategies and maximize your wealth growth.

Automatic Disbursements

Automatic disbursements are scheduled transfers or payments that occur automatically. Use them to model recurring transactions between accounts or payments to external parties.

Setting Up Automatic Disbursements

  1. Go to Settings > Automatic Transfers
  2. Navigate to the "Automatic Disbursements" tab
  3. Click "Add Disbursement" or "+ New Disbursement"
  4. Configure the disbursement:
    • Description: Name for the disbursement
    • Source Account: Account that pays
    • Target Account or Expense: Where the money goes
    • Amount: How much to transfer/pay
    • Frequency: How often it occurs
    • Start Date: When it begins
    • End Date: (Optional) When it stops
  5. Click "Save"

Types of Disbursements

  • Account Transfers: Move money between your accounts
  • Expense Payments: Automatic payments for expenses (may link to expense items)
  • Investment Contributions: Regular contributions to investment accounts
  • Debt Payments: Scheduled debt payments (beyond minimum payments)

How It Works

Automatic disbursements:

  • Execute on their scheduled frequency
  • Affect cash flow by moving money between accounts or to expenses
  • Can be linked to specific expense items to track their funding
  • Show up in cash flow projections

Managing Disbursements

  • Edit: Click the edit icon to modify a disbursement

Impact on Projections

Automatic disbursements affect:

Cash Assets

  • Pay for expenses

  • Set which accounts income sources deposit to
  • Configure default accounts for new income items

Cash-Out Destinations

  • Set which accounts expense items are paid from
  • Configure default accounts for new expense items

  • Configure cash-out destination accounts

  • Cash flow projections accurately reflect where money goes

  • Automatic disbursements target the right accounts

Connected Accounts (Plaid)

Connect your bank, credit card, and investment accounts through Plaid to automatically import account information and transactions.

What is Plaid?

Plaid is a secure service that connects your financial accounts to Financial Projector. It allows you to:

  • Import account balances automatically
  • Sync transaction data
  • Stay up to date without manual entry
  • Connect accounts from thousands of financial institutions

Connecting an Account

  1. Go to Settings > Accounts
  2. Click "Connect Account" or "Link via Plaid"
  3. Search for your financial institution
  4. Select the institution and log in with your credentials
  5. Select which accounts to connect
  6. Grant permissions for Financial Projector to access account data
  7. Accounts will be imported and appear in your account list

Supported Account Types

  • Banking: Checking and savings accounts
  • Credit Cards: Credit card accounts
  • Investments: Brokerage and investment accounts
  • Loans: Mortgages, auto loans, student loans

What Gets Imported

  • Account Balances: Current account balances
  • Account Names: Account names from your institution
  • Account Types: Classification of account types
  • Mortgage Details: Loan terms, payment schedules (for mortgages)
  • Transaction History: Recent transactions (if supported)

Managing Connected Accounts

  • Refresh: Manually refresh account balances and data
  • Reconnect: If connection fails, reconnect the account
  • Disconnect: Remove the Plaid connection (account data remains)
  • Auto-Sync: Accounts may sync automatically on a schedule

Security and Privacy

  • Plaid uses bank-level security and encryption
  • Your credentials are never stored by Financial Projector
  • Plaid uses read-only access (cannot move money)
  • You can disconnect accounts at any time
  • Data is encrypted in transit and at rest

Auto-Generated Items from Plaid

When you connect accounts via Plaid, the system may automatically create:

  • Assets: Investment accounts as assets
  • Liabilities: Mortgage and loan accounts as liabilities
  • Expenses: Mortgage payment expenses (principal and interest)
  • Accounts: Account entries in your account list

Troubleshooting

  • Connection Failed: Verify your credentials and try again
  • Account Not Found: Some institutions may not be supported - check Plaid's supported institutions list
  • Stale Data: Refresh the account manually or wait for automatic sync
  • Multiple Logins Required: Some institutions require multi-factor authentication - follow the prompts
Tip: Connected accounts keep your data up to date automatically. However, you can still manually edit account balances and details if needed. Connected accounts are especially useful for tracking mortgage payments and investment account balances.

Export / Import

Export your data for backup or analysis, and import data from other sources. This feature helps you maintain data portability and work with your financial data in other tools.

Exporting Data

  1. Go to Settings > Export / Import
  2. Click "Export Data" or "Download Backup"
  3. Select what to export:
    • All data (assets, liabilities, income, expenses, settings)
    • Specific categories (just assets, just income, etc.)
    • Settings only
  4. Choose export format (JSON, CSV, Excel)
  5. Click "Download"

What Gets Exported

  • Assets: All asset entries with details
  • Liabilities: All liability entries with details
  • Income: All income sources with amounts and frequencies
  • Expenses: All expense items with amounts and frequencies
  • Accounts: Account configurations
  • Settings: User settings, categories, preferences
  • Charts: Custom chart configurations (optional)

Importing Data

  1. Go to Settings > Export / Import
  2. Click "Import Data" or "Upload File"
  3. Select the file to import (must match export format)
  4. Choose import options:
    • Merge: Add imported data to existing data
    • Replace: Replace existing data with imported data
    • Update Only: Update existing items, don't add new ones
  5. Review the import preview (if available)
  6. Click "Import" to proceed

Import Formats

  • JSON: Complete data structure with all details (recommended for full backups)
  • CSV: Spreadsheet format, good for bulk data entry
  • Excel: Excel workbook format

Use Cases

  • Backup: Create regular backups of your financial data
  • Analysis: Export data to analyze in Excel, Google Sheets, or other tools
  • Migration: Move data between accounts or systems
  • Bulk Entry: Import large amounts of data from spreadsheets
  • Sharing: Share data with advisors or family (with proper permissions)

Best Practices

  • Export data regularly as backups
  • Test imports with a small file first
  • Review imported data after importing
  • Keep exported files in a secure location
  • Use JSON format for complete backups
  • Use CSV/Excel for bulk data entry
Warning: Importing data with "Replace" option will delete your existing data. Always export a backup before importing with replace option. Review the import preview carefully before confirming.
Tip: Regular exports serve as a backup in case of data loss. Consider exporting monthly or before making major changes to your data. Store backups securely as they contain sensitive financial information.
